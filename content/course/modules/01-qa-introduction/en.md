# Introduction to QA

Manual testing is careful investigation of a product through real use, requirements, risks, and evidence. A tester does not only click through screens. A tester asks what the product is meant to do, what can go wrong, which risks matter most, and how to explain findings to the team.

## A day in the life of a manual tester

On a typical product team, a manual QA engineer may:

- read a requirement or user story and ask clarifying questions;
- turn requirements into checklists, test ideas, or test cases;
- test new changes on a prepared environment;
- compare actual behavior with expected behavior;
- report defects with clear steps, evidence, impact, and context;
- retest fixes and update the team about remaining risk.

Testing work changes as the release gets closer. Early in development, QA helps clarify behavior and risk. Near release, QA focuses on evidence, regression risk, and whether the team understands what remains uncertain.

## Testing and quality

Testing shows information about product quality. It can reveal defects, reduce risk, and support better decisions. It cannot prove that a product has no bugs.

Two useful terms:

- **Quality Control (QC)** checks a product or feature to find defects and confirm whether it meets expectations.
- **Quality Assurance (QA)** improves the process that creates the product, so quality is built in earlier and more consistently.

In real teams, a QA engineer often contributes to both: checking the product and improving the way the team works.

## Core testing principles

Keep these ideas in mind while working with an AI tutor:

1. Testing shows the presence of defects, not their absence.
2. Exhaustive testing is impossible, so testers prioritize by risk and value.
3. Early testing reduces the cost of misunderstandings and fixes.
4. Defects often cluster in complex, changing, or poorly understood areas.
5. Repeating the same checks forever can miss new problems.
6. Testing depends on context: a banking feature, a game, and a landing page need different strategies.
7. A product with no visible defects can still fail if it does not solve the user's problem.

## Working with the team

Manual QA interacts with product managers, analysts, designers, frontend and backend developers, DevOps engineers, support, and other testers. Each role sees a different part of the product. QA connects these perspectives by asking precise questions and turning answers into useful checks.

Good QA communication is specific and calm: describe what happened, where it happened, why it matters, and what evidence supports it.

## Soft skills for QA

Useful QA soft skills include curiosity, attention to detail, clear writing, structured thinking, teamwork, and willingness to learn. The most important beginner habit is simple: when something is unclear, turn it into a question before turning it into a test.
